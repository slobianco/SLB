# Cielo Rojo — Project Context

> Authoritative creative and content context for this repository, provided by Santiago Lo Bianco.
> This is the single source of truth for the Cielo Rojo project identity, narrative, visual
> direction, and content rules. `AGENTS.md` points here rather than duplicating this content.

## 1. Purpose of these instructions

This repository contains the official website for **Cielo Rojo**, a personal music project by
**Santiago Lo Bianco**.

This document is the authoritative project context for any coding agent designing, writing,
refactoring, testing, or reviewing this website.

The developer/owner of this repository is learning web development, GitHub Copilot, and VS Code.
Therefore:

- Prefer clear, maintainable, and understandable solutions over clever or unnecessarily
  sophisticated ones.
- Explain significant architectural changes before implementing them.
- Avoid introducing new frameworks, libraries, or abstractions without a clear need.
- Make incremental changes rather than rewriting large areas of the application unnecessarily.
- Preserve working functionality unless a change explicitly requires replacing it.
- When proposing commands, explain briefly what they do.
- When debugging, identify the likely cause before making broad changes.
- When making substantial changes, test the relevant user journey afterwards.
- Do not assume the repository owner understands advanced web-development terminology. Explain
  unfamiliar concepts plainly when relevant.

## 2. Project identity

**Artist:** Santiago Lo Bianco
**Album:** Cielo Rojo

The internal repository/project may sometimes be referred to as **Glebe Rock – Cielo Rojo**, but
Glebe Rock should not automatically be presented as the public artist name.

Unless explicitly changed by the owner, the public artist identity is **Santiago Lo Bianco** and
the album is **Cielo Rojo**.

Do not invent alternative artist names, bands, record labels, or collaborators.

## 3. What Cielo Rojo is

Cielo Rojo is an independent, deeply personal rock album and one-man-band project.

It is guitar-driven rock with influences that can cross rock, alternative rock, punk, progressive
rock, metal, and melodic/pop elements depending on the song. It should not be presented as
belonging rigidly to one narrow genre.

The project is centred on Santiago's own songwriting, lyrics, melodies, musical ideas, guitar and
bass performance, arrangement decisions, production decisions, creative direction, artwork
direction, and overall concept.

The project also incorporates modern digital and AI-assisted music-production techniques. Some
generated or AI-assisted musical elements have been used during production alongside Santiago's
own performances. Public-facing descriptions of exactly how these tools were used are **not yet
finalised**. Therefore:

- Do not invent production credits.
- Do not claim that every sound was manually performed.
- Do not claim that the album was entirely AI-generated.
- Do not make AI the central narrative of the project unless specifically requested.
- Do not write detailed public explanations of AI involvement unless Santiago provides or
  approves the wording.

The fundamental creative principle is that **Cielo Rojo is Santiago's work and artistic journey,
with technology serving the music rather than defining it.**

## 4. Album narrative

Cielo Rojo should feel like **a journey** rather than merely a collection of tracks. Recurring
ideas include: time, memory, change, identity, anxiety, loss, absence, self-reflection, dreams,
relationships, distance, choices, becoming someone different, looking backwards, moving forwards,
accepting what cannot be recovered, and recognising what remains.

The emotional world should feel introspective rather than theatrical.

Avoid generic rock-marketing clichés such as "legendary," "explosive new artist," "groundbreaking
rock sensation," "taking the world by storm," "an unstoppable musical force," "epic sonic
assault," or "decade of fire" unless Santiago explicitly approves them.

Cielo Rojo should sound like a human story, not a record-company press release.

## 6. Lyrics, track meanings, and Echoes

Each song may eventually include lyrics, a short description, the story behind the song, artwork,
production notes, credits, and an **Echo** — a reflective line associated with the track.

The Echo concept was developed for the physical album/booklet. Echoes are short thoughts or
questions left with the listener after a song; many intentionally take the form of questions. This
is deliberate — they should feel reflective rather than promotional.

Do not invent Echoes, lyrics, song meanings, or quotations. Use only material explicitly provided
in the repository or supplied by Santiago. If content has not yet been provided, use an obvious
development placeholder such as `TODO: Add approved track description` rather than fabricating
content.

## 7. Visual identity

The website must belong to the same visual universe as the Cielo Rojo album artwork:

- a red or dark-red sky
- horizon imagery, roads, distance, silhouettes, cinematic landscapes
- darkness contrasted with red light
- smoke, haze, or atmospheric depth; subtle grain
- cracked asphalt or earth; time imagery
- a damaged or broken watch/clock; fragments
- a feeling of travelling towards or away from something
- melancholy without becoming gothic; cinematic rather than glossy
- mature rather than teenage; restrained rather than visually chaotic

The **broken clock/watch** is one of the most important recurring visual motifs, representing
time, memory, things that cannot be recovered, change, interruption, and fragments of the past. Use
it thoughtfully rather than placing clocks everywhere.

## 8. Front-cover visual direction

The album-cover universe has explored a figure looking towards a distant horizon beneath the red
sky. Recent creative work also explores incorporating a real photograph of Santiago into this
visual language.

Important characteristics: cinematic composition, strong horizon, restrained red, dark
foreground, atmospheric distance, broken-watch symbolism, distressed or strong condensed
typography, and a sense that the viewer is looking into a journey.

The website should ultimately use approved real artwork/assets rather than generic stock concert
photography where practical. Do not redesign the album cover or artist logo unless explicitly
requested.

## 9. Back-cover narrative

The back cover complements rather than duplicates the front cover, exploring the journey from the
opposite perspective: the road behind, a traveller becoming distant, the red light fading, darker
blue/purple atmosphere, traces of what has happened, the journey reaching a conclusion.

This reflects an important principle for the website too: **repeated visual elements should
evolve as the visitor moves through the experience rather than simply repeating the same image.**

## 10. Branding

The website currently uses the Cielo Rojo name prominently. Previous branding experiments include
an **SLB** identity and a preferred broken/fragmented logo direction sometimes referred to during
design work as **Broken Meridian**.

Do not automatically create another logo. Until approved final branding assets are available,
favour clean typographic presentation, use supplied artwork when available, avoid inventing
record-label style logos, and keep branding consistent across desktop and mobile.

## 11. Colour and atmosphere

The site should generally feel dark, cinematic, and textured. The core visual relationship is
between near-black/charcoal, off-white, muted greys, restrained deep reds, and occasional
atmospheric blue/purple tones.

Red should be an accent and emotional anchor rather than covering every surface. Avoid bright
generic corporate reds and excessive gradients. The interface must remain legible and accessible —
visual atmosphere must never make text difficult to read.

## 12. Typography

The established artwork direction favours strong, condensed, somewhat distressed or industrial
typography for major titles. For the website: headings can have character, but body copy must
remain highly readable. Do not use distressed fonts for long paragraphs. Typography should feel
contemporary, cinematic, and rock-oriented without becoming a heavy-metal parody. Consistency is
more important than using many font styles.

## 13. Website purpose

The website is intended to become the **official home of Cielo Rojo**. The current prototype is
exploratory. Many current sections, labels, images, and pieces of copy are generic placeholders
and should NOT be interpreted as factual information about Santiago or the album.

The website should eventually allow visitors to: discover the Cielo Rojo project; understand the
concept and journey behind the album; explore the album and its tracks; read lyrics and approved
track stories; listen to or follow links to streaming platforms when available; explore artwork
and visual material; learn about Santiago and the making of the project; see future news or
project updates; access future physical-album or merchandise information if it becomes available;
and switch naturally between English and Spanish.

The website should feel like an **album experience first and a commercial website second**.

## 14. Current navigation

The prototype currently explores navigation such as Home, Story, Music, Tour, Merch, News, and
Contact. These sections are NOT all confirmed requirements. In particular:

- **Tour** — there are currently no approved tour dates. Never invent performances, venues,
  cities, or dates. Use a "coming soon" treatment until real information exists.
- **Merch** — no merchandise catalogue, pricing, or store has been approved yet. Never invent
  products or prices.
- **News** — do not fabricate news articles, release announcements, reviews, interviews, or
  dates.
- **Contact** — do not invent management companies, record labels, agents, email addresses,
  phone numbers, or booking contacts. Use only approved information.

## 15. Absolutely no fabricated content

This is one of the most important project rules. When real content is unavailable, do NOT
generate believable fictional information merely to make the website look complete. This includes
biographies, dates, song-release dates, album-release dates, concert dates, venues, streaming
statistics, quotations, reviews, awards, press coverage, collaborators, record labels, producers,
studios, addresses, email addresses, social-media accounts, prices, merchandise, lyrics, track
descriptions, song meanings, and musical credits.

Instead use a clearly marked placeholder, a TODO, an empty state, or content explicitly described
as prototype/demo content. When uncertain, ask Santiago rather than inventing facts.

## 16. Placeholder-content rule

Prototype content is allowed when developing layout, but it must be visibly distinguishable in the
source code as placeholder content. Prefer `TODO: Replace with approved biography` over realistic
fictional biographies. Prefer "Coming soon" over invented tour dates. Prefer "Streaming links will
be added when released" over fake Spotify/Apple Music URLs.

## 17. English and Spanish

The website is bilingual: English and Spanish. The project already uses locale-based routing —
preserve this architecture unless there is a compelling technical reason to change it.

Translations must be natural rather than literal. English should use **British English** where a
regional spelling difference exists. Spanish should sound natural for an Argentine artist and
should not automatically use Spain-specific expressions.

Track names must NOT be translated unless an official translated title is explicitly provided
(e.g. Silent Memory remains Silent Memory; La Desolación remains La Desolación; Ansiedad remains
Ansiedad).

## 18. Mobile-first requirement

The site must work properly on mobile, tablet, laptop, and desktop. Every major change should be
checked at representative mobile and desktop widths. Do not treat mobile as a compressed desktop
layout — navigation must remain usable, typography must not overflow, buttons must remain
comfortably clickable, artwork focal points should remain visible, spacing should be sensible, and
horizontal scrolling should not occur accidentally.

## 19. Accessibility

Cinematic design must not compromise accessibility. Maintain semantic HTML, sensible heading
hierarchy, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, sufficient colour contrast, meaningful
alternative text for meaningful images, decorative-image handling where appropriate, labelled
controls, and appropriate button/link semantics.

## 20. Motion and animation

Motion should support atmosphere: subtle image movement, gentle parallax, controlled fades, slow
atmospheric transitions, restrained hover effects, subtle clock/time-related animation, small
visual reveals while scrolling. Avoid excessive animation, constant movement, gimmicky cursor
effects, aggressive zooms, and long entrance animations that delay content. The user should never
have to wait for an animation before normal navigation works. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 21. Spotify Canvas / video philosophy

The wider Cielo Rojo project has explored simple cinematic video and Spotify Canvas concepts. The
preferred philosophy is subtle, repeatable, atmospheric, achievable without expensive filmmaking,
and visually connected to the album universe.

## 22. Physical album / booklet

Cielo Rojo is also being developed with physical artwork in mind: front cover, back cover, digipak,
booklet, tracklist, song lyrics, Echoes/reflections, and visual storytelling between panels. Future
website sections may reuse or reinterpret this material without assuming print artwork can simply
be placed unchanged onto a webpage.

## 23. Development architecture

This repository uses a modern Next.js/TypeScript structure, including locale-based routing under
`app/[locale]`, `components`, `content`, `i18n`, `messages`, `public`, and `styles`. Preserve the
existing architecture unless there is a clear reason to change it. Before introducing a new
library: check whether the requirement can reasonably be implemented with the current stack,
explain why the dependency is needed, prefer established/maintained packages, and avoid adding a
dependency for something trivial.

## 24. Content architecture

Content and presentation should remain reasonably separated. Album metadata should live in central
content/data files, track information should have a single source of truth, navigation labels and
translated copy should use the existing localisation system, and components should not contain
large duplicated blocks of album data. If the album order changes, ideally only one source of
truth should need updating.

## 25. Images and media

Do not permanently rely on random stock photos merely because they suit the current layout.
During prototyping, generic imagery may temporarily demonstrate a component — mark it as temporary
when appropriate. Approved Cielo Rojo artwork, photography, and logos should eventually replace
generic imagery. When modifying image behaviour: preserve aspect ratio, avoid unnecessary
distortion, consider mobile cropping, use responsive image techniques, avoid unnecessarily large
files, and preserve important focal points. Do not alter approved artwork itself unless explicitly
requested.

## 26. Code quality

Prefer TypeScript types, small understandable components, descriptive names, reusable components
where reuse is genuinely useful, straightforward CSS, and existing project conventions. Avoid
premature abstraction, unnecessary design patterns, giant components, duplicated album content,
unexplained magic numbers, disabling TypeScript/lint rules to silence an error, and extensive
refactoring unrelated to the requested feature. When fixing a bug, make the smallest reliable
change that addresses the root cause.

## 27. Testing and verification

After implementing functionality, verify it: test actual navigation for navigation changes; test
English, Spanish, switching between both, and URL locale behaviour for localisation changes; test
representative mobile and desktop viewports for responsive changes; test actual clicks (not just
source code) for interactive controls. Do not report something as working unless it was reasonably
verified.

## 28. Protect working behaviour

Do not replace major existing implementations simply because another approach is more fashionable.
Before a broad refactor: understand the current implementation, identify the concrete benefit,
explain what will change, and preserve current user-facing behaviour unless the change is
intentional. If a task can be solved safely in five files, do not modify fifty files.

## 29. Working with a beginner developer

Santiago is intentionally using this project to learn. When completing substantial work: briefly
explain what was changed, identify the important files, explain any new concept introduced, and
mention how Santiago can verify the result himself. Do not overwhelm him with every implementation
detail — focus on concepts that help him become progressively more independent. If there are
several valid approaches, recommend one and explain why rather than presenting many alternatives.

## 30. Git and change safety

Before destructive or wide-ranging modifications: inspect the current state, avoid deleting
unrelated work, preserve existing assets, and keep changes focused on the requested task. Do not
perform destructive Git operations unless explicitly requested. Do not silently discard existing
modifications. When a change affects many files, summarise why each major group of changes was
necessary.

## 31. Design decision hierarchy

1. Cielo Rojo's established artistic identity
2. Clarity and usability
3. Mobile responsiveness
4. Accessibility
5. Consistency
6. Performance
7. Visual novelty

Never sacrifice usability merely to make something look impressive.

## 32. Content decision hierarchy

1. Explicitly approved Cielo Rojo information
2. Repository content identified as authoritative
3. Clear placeholders
4. Asking Santiago for missing information

Fabrication is not an acceptable fifth option.

## 33. Tone of public-facing copy

The writing should generally feel personal, mature, introspective, cinematic, direct, emotionally
intelligent, understated, and human. It may occasionally be poetic, but it should not become
pretentious. Avoid excessive marketing language — short sentences can be powerful. Allow the music
and imagery to do some of the work.

## 34. Development workflow for substantial requests

1. Inspect the relevant existing files.
2. Explain the proposed approach briefly.
3. Identify likely files to change.
4. Implement the smallest coherent version.
5. Run appropriate checks/tests.
6. Test the user journey.
7. Report what changed.
8. Mention anything still using placeholder data.

For a simple small fix, do not turn the task into an architectural exercise.

## 35. Current project phase

The website is currently a **prototype**. The technical structure and generic sections are being
developed before the real Cielo Rojo content is fully inserted. The immediate focus is:
establishing a solid site structure, learning the development workflow, validating responsive
behaviour, validating English/Spanish localisation, establishing reusable components, and
replacing generic content progressively with authentic Cielo Rojo material. Do not interpret
unfinished content as a problem to be solved by inventing content — real album material will be
incorporated progressively.

## 36. Guiding principle

When making any decision about this website, remember: **this is not a generic rock-band template
with Cielo Rojo written on top of it.** The goal is to build a digital extension of the album
itself. The website should gradually become another part of the Cielo Rojo journey.
